## Primary & Supplementary Textbooks & References

1. **John R. Taylor** — *Classical Mechanics* (University Science Books, 2005)
   * Primary course textbook for PHYS 3309. Outstanding step-by-step mathematical pedagogy and end-of-chapter problems.

2. **Richard P. Feynman, Robert B. Leighton, Matthew Sands** — *The Feynman Lectures on Physics*, Vol. 1
   * The definitive conceptual foundation for physics students. Key recommended chapters:
     * **Ch. 8–10**: Description of motion, Newton's dynamical laws, and momentum conservation.
     * **Ch. 11**: Vectors and polar coordinates.
     * **Ch. 13–14**: Work, potential energy, conservative fields, and work-energy theorem.
     * **Ch. 18–20**: Center of mass, rotation, and angular momentum.
     * **Ch. 22**: Algebra, complex numbers, and simple harmonic oscillations.
     * **Ch. 41**: Viscous flow, Stoke's drag, and fluid friction.

3. **Daniel Kleppner & Robert Kolenkow** — *An Introduction to Mechanics* (Cambridge University Press)
   * The benchmark for rigorous classical problem-solving; invaluable companion for drag forces, variable-mass systems (rockets), and non-inertial frames.

4. **David Morin** — *Introduction to Classical Mechanics: With Problems and Solutions* (Cambridge University Press)
   * Highly recommended for limbering up mathematical mechanics; rich collection of worked problems, 1D potential wells, and Euler-Lagrange applications.

5. **L.D. Landau & E.M. Lifshitz** — *Mechanics (Course of Theoretical Physics, Vol. 1)*
   * The quintessential theoretical treatise on least action, coordinate symmetries, and Noether's conservation laws.
