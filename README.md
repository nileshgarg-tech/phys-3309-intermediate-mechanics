# PHYS 3309 — Intermediate Mechanics
**Textbook**: *Classical Mechanics* (2005) by John R. Taylor

---

## Quick Book Overview

The textbook is divided into two logical sections:
* **Part 1: Essentials (Chapters 1–11)**: Core undergraduate mechanics curriculum.
* **Part 2: Further Topics (Chapters 12–16)**: Advanced topics, modern physics, and computational extensions.

## Part 1: Essentials (Core Junior Mechanics)

| Chapter | Title | Pages | Summary in Plain Terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Newton's Laws of Motion** | 3–42 | Reference frames, Newton's 3 laws, momentum conservation, and vectors in 2D polar coordinates (`r`, `θ`). |
| **2** | **Projectiles & Charged Particles** | 43–82 | Trajectories with linear and quadratic air drag, terminal velocity, and charge motion in magnetic fields. |
| **3** | **Momentum & Angular Momentum** | 83–113 | Multi-particle systems, Center of Mass (CM), rocket propulsion (variable mass), and angular momentum. |

| **6** | **Calculus of Variations** | 215–236 | Optimization of paths (e.g. shortest time/distance) using the Euler-Lagrange variational equation. |
| **7** | **Lagrange's Equations** | 237–292 | Solving complex mechanics using kinetic & potential energy with generalized coordinates (`q_i`) instead of forces. |
| **8** | **Two-Body Central Forces** | 293–326 | Planetary orbits, Kepler's laws, reduced mass (`μ`), effective potential `U_eff(r)`, and orbital transfers. |
| **9** | **Non-Inertial Frames** | 327–366 | Physics in accelerating and rotating frames: centrifugal force, Coriolis effect on free fall, and Foucault pendulum. |
| **10** | **Rotational Motion of Rigid Bodies** | 367–416 | 3D rotations, inertia tensor `I`, principal axes, Euler's equations, torque-free precession, and spinning tops. |
| **11** | **Coupled Oscillators & Normal Modes** | 417–456 | Multiple connected springs and pendulums, matrix methods for finding natural frequencies and normal modes. |

## Primary & Recommended Course References

1. **John R. Taylor** — *Classical Mechanics* (University Science Books, 2005)
   * The primary course textbook for UH PHYS 3309. Known for unmatched pedagogical clarity and thorough derivations.
2. **Richard P. Feynman, Robert B. Leighton, Matthew Sands** — *The Feynman Lectures on Physics*, Vol. 1
   * The premier conceptual reference. Key companion chapters:
     * **Ch. 8–10**: Kinematics, Newton's laws, and momentum conservation.
     * **Ch. 11**: Vectors and curvilinear motion.
     * **Ch. 13–14**: Work, kinetic energy, potential wells, and conservation principles.
     * **Ch. 18–20**: Center of mass, rotation, and angular momentum.
     * **Ch. 22**: Complex algebra and oscillatory motion.
     * **Ch. 41**: Fluid resistance, viscosity, and Stokes drag.
3. **Daniel Kleppner & Robert Kolenkow** — *An Introduction to Mechanics* (Cambridge University Press)
   * The gold-standard problem-solving reference; exceptional depth on polar coordinates, drag, variable mass (rockets), and fictitious forces.
4. **David Morin** — *Introduction to Classical Mechanics: With Problems and Solutions* (Cambridge University Press)
   * Superb for challenging practice problems, 1D potential wells, and Lagrangian mechanics.
5. **L.D. Landau & E.M. Lifshitz** — *Mechanics (Course of Theoretical Physics, Vol. 1)*
   * The classic theoretical benchmark for least action, symmetry principles, and conserved quantities.
